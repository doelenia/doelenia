# Doelenian grammar

A small grammar, taken from the founder's notes and held still so it can grow without changing its bones.

The speech is toward what is true in this world. A long claim stays one sentence. It does not drop a claim, and it does not break the sentence into a row of short ones.

## Sounds

Syllables are open (`doe`, `au`, `ne`) or closed by one consonant (`len`, `hax`, `weyat`). Vowels are a, e, i, o, u. A word does not end in r. The flowing pronouns begin in y instead of j: `yeh`, `yan`, `yen`.

A capital marks a holy noun that stands bare, a finished name, or the first word of a sentence. `Jah` is god, so it stands bare and keeps the capital. A common noun does not stand bare. It takes a picture and stays lower: `auzen`, `auna`, `navax`. A negation that has become its own word takes a hyphen: `nun-leh`.

## The founder's pairs

Several words come in two bodies. The short one combines or speaks quickly. The long one stands alone.

| Short | Long | Role |
|-------|------|------|
| se | sees | of the stars / the stars |
| ha | hax | of an activity / the activity |
| va | vax | of a field / the field of work |
| ne | nes | is, clipped / is, in a full sentence |
| to | — | of, in common speech |
| no | — | the old of, after a name. Not used inside a name |
| ai, o, yeh, yan, yen | jai, joo, jeh, jan, jen | pronouns, flowing / pronouns, opening |

`to` is of, 的. Possessor first (`sees to vax`, `auzen to navax`). A name is its words in order, with nothing between them: `Doelenia Ommia Kazen`. `no` is not placed inside a name.

`nes` is the copula of a spoken sentence. `ne` is the same word when a line is a name or a chant and has no room for the full body.

## Word order

Possessor first, then the particle, then the thing possessed.

> sees to vax — the field of the stars
> Doelenia Ommia Kazen — the name, with no particle between the words
> auzen to navax — the ark of humanity

A defining sentence is Subject, then `nes`, then what it is.

> Haseva nes sees to vax.
> Haseva is the field of the stars.

An adjective follows its noun.

> hax weyata — a joyful work
> weyat — joy, the thing
> weyata — joyful, the quality (`-a` on the noun)

## What gets its own word

English, at the start, gave a simple word to a simple thing, and built later words out of those.

A thing that is fundamental or holy gets its own root. Ark is holy, so its root is `nax`. Human is holy, so its root is `au`. The root is not yet the noun. The phrase stays a phrase of two nouns, each one finished by its picture.

> auzen to navax — the ark for humanity

Truth, life, world, belief, love, knowledge, an order, a path: each of these is a root. They are not fused with their neighbors just because the sentence says them often. Each one becomes a noun only when a picture is set on it.

A word keeps one meaning a reader can recognize. If a second use would blur the first, the second use gets its own word. `doe` is alone. It is not the act of closing. `lun` is a menu. `suh` shuts what is open.

A thing that is not fundamental is a compound. The stem of the qualifier joins the head. The word stays lowercase, because it is not a special name.

> mei + ia = meia, a platform, a ground that was made
> vin + sna = vinsna, the trace by which one joins

`sih` remains the root of an order. The noun is `sivax`. One's own system is `doe ia to sivax`: the words stay separate. A renaissance and a system stay two nouns, `rehsna to sivax`.

A finished name the founder already gave stays a name, and keeps its capital: `Ausna`, `Seesoun`, `Doelenia`. `auzen` is the common noun for humanity. The capital `Auzen` is that same noun when it is the founder's name. A new phrase does not become a name by being repeated.

`nun` stands before the word it denies.

> auna nun ken — a human does not know
> kensna nun len — knowledge does not depart

A verb does not change for person or time. The ritual present is the only tense. Subject, then verb, then object.

> jan sen lehsna — we seek truth
> jan len sna to mahia — we send the trace into the world

## Joining claims

Doelenian sits between English and Chinese. Chinese can set two claims side by side and let the reader supply the relation. English gives each relation its own clause shape, and often a pronoun to go with it. Doelenian speaks a relation with one word when English would use a word, and uses the same marks English uses when the job is punctuation.

The marks are the period, the comma, the dash, the semicolon, and the parentheses. They are not said as words. A comma marks the clause that specifies the noun just spoken. There is no article, and no agreement.

The subject is spoken when it changes. When the next claim has the same subject, the subject stays unspoken.

| Word | Place | Meaning |
|------|--------|---------|
| eh | X eh Y | and. The word, where English says and. A list's commas are not this word |
| bah | , bah | but |
| yoh | , yoh | because. The same word as the cause |
| nel | , nel | so that. The same word as the aim |
| hem | , hem | while; in the same act |

> nes auzen to navax, sen rehsna to sivax, vel auzen to povax.
> is the ark of humanity that seeks a renaissance system that runs on the potential of humanity.

> Jan nes kazen, bah nun nes meia.
> We are an alliance, but we are not a platform.

> Vem auzen to shasna, hem yut reihax, nel hax vel sna.
> Take in the feeling of humanity while using reason, so that the act carries wisdom.

## The noun picture

Doelenian is a noun-focused language. A root names the stuff. It is not a noun. A noun is the root with a picture fused at the end. The picture is itself a root that has become a norm: the same few roots finish almost every noun, and the picture says what kind of noun it is.

| Picture | Norm | Noun |
|---------|------|------|
| sna | a trace; what is left | lehsna, truth. kensna, knowledge. tahsna, writings. Ausna is this picture on au |
| na | one being | auna, one human. renna, one living being |
| ia | a ground | mahia, the world. meia, a made ground |
| ian | a person of a ground, or the language | ian. Doelenian |
| vax | a space; a container | navax, the ark. sivax, a system. wevax, a book |
| hax | an activity; a work | reihax, reason. tahax, a conversation |
| oun | a company | the group itself may be spoken `oun` |
| zen | an integrated whole, and only that | auzen, humanity. hunzen, the whole of a life. renzen, living beings as one whole |

`zen` is not the ordinary picture. It is the heavy whole, heavier than `oun`, and it is used when the noun really is one body. Truth, a book, a menu, a path, and a feeling do not take it.

`sna` is the ordinary picture for what a thing leaves behind. It is the ending of Ausna. A writing, a memory, a vow, and a belief take `sna`. `wen` is the root of a book. It is not an ending for other nouns. The book itself is `wevax`, a container of traces.

A common noun is one root and one light picture. It is not a stack. About is `lehia`: the root `leh`, and the light end `ia`. It is not `doelenlehsna`. When a meaning needs two roots, they stay two words.

When the root's last consonant and the picture's first consonant jam, the root shortens. It drops that consonant. The picture stays whole, so the ending can be read at a glance.

> nax + vax → navax
> sih + vax → sivax
> wen + vax → wevax
> tah + hax → tahax
> koh + hax → kohax

A join that already flows stays whole. `ora` ends in a vowel, so `oravax`. `leh` before `sna` is `lehsna`, the Ausna shape.

`-a` remains the adjective (`weyata`). It is not `na`. `auna` is `au` + `na`, one human.

A picture may be spoken alone when the noun is nothing but that picture: `oun` is a company, `sna` is a trace, `zen` is the whole, `ia` is a ground, `hax` is a work. A content root may not. `au`, `nax`, `leh`, `sih`, `wen` are not nouns.

A root may be spoken bare in one other place: when the sentence names the root itself.

> au nes au — the root au is human
> sna nes sna — the root sna is the trace

Almost no noun stands as a bare root. The exception is a holy noun. It stands bare, and it is capitalized, so the eye can see that the exception was taken.

> Jah — god

A finished name is the same exception. It was given complete, it stands, and it is capitalized: `Doelenia`, `Ausna`, `Ommia`, `Kazen`, `Minavax`. `kazen` in ordinary speech already carries `zen` inside the name. It stays lower when it is not the name.

The picture is fused once, at the end of one root, and it is chosen for the kind of noun, the way Ausna is human plus trace. A word that already ends in a picture is finished (`meia`, `hunzen`, `kohax`). Two holy nouns stay two words. Humanity and the ark are not fused.

> auzen to navax

## Building words

**-ia** is a ground, a region. **-ian** is a person of that ground, or the language spoken there.

> doe + len + ia = Doelenia, the ground of those who go forth alone
> Doelenian = a person of that ground, and this language

**oun** is a company, 团: people bound by one work.
**zen** is an integrated body, 集成体: many become one thing. A zen is heavier than an oun.

The noun form stands when the word is alone (`sees`, `hax`). The stem is what enters a compound (`se`, `ha`). A long compound may be spoken shorter: `oun` becomes `on`.

| Word | Build | Gloss |
|------|--------|--------|
| Seesoun | sees + oun | the company of the stars |
| Seeoun | se + oun | the same company, on the stem |
| Haseoun | ha + se + oun | the company of stellar work |
| Haseon | Haseoun, spoken short | the same, in chant |
| auzen | au + zen | humanity, the human as one body |
| auna | au + na | one human |
| Ausna | au + sna | humanity, and the wisdom it leaves |
| Haseva | ha + se + va | the stellar field, in stem form |

**au** is the root for human. **auzen** is humanity. **auna** is one human. **sna** is the trace a human leaves, and it is the picture for what is left. That is the founder's gloss of Ausna: 人性与智慧. The manifesto's "traces we leave" and this gloss are one word.

## Pronouns

These are persons, as the notes say （人）. The long form opens a sentence. The short form may follow.

| | Long | Short |
|--|------|--------|
| I | jai | ai |
| you | joo | o |
| he, she | jeh | yeh |
| we | jan | yan |
| they | jen | yen |

> jai to wevax — my book
> jan nes kazen — we are a covenant

## The model sentence

Keep this line as the test of the grammar. If a new rule cannot read it, the rule is wrong.

> Haseva nes sees to vax.

Haseva (the stellar field, spoken as a stem) is the field of the stars.

## What may be added

A new root is allowed when the thing is fundamental or holy and no word yet names it. Mark it `new`. If the thing is not fundamental, compound it from roots already in the lexicon and mark it `derived`. Do not add a new particle unless `nes`, `ne`, `to`, `no`, `nun`, `eh`, `bah`, `yoh`, `nel`, `ke`, and `hem` cannot say the thing.

The glossary is `glossary.html` in this folder, separate from the website. It is searched by name or by meaning. Edit `lexicon.ts`, then run `node language/build-glossary.mjs`.
